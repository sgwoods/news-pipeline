Write the complete spoken script for today's episode of the AI Briefing podcast, from the briefing sections below.

The episode and the written briefing are different products for overlapping audiences. A reader skims the written edition, jumps around and follows links. A listener hears the episode once, in order, often while driving, and cannot see a list, a link or a number on a page. So the script is not the briefing read aloud. It is a linear piece of radio. Tell the listener where they are ("the first of six stories", "two quick ones before the questions"), carry them from one story to the next with a real transition, and briefly connect stories when they share a thread. Some listeners are not Steven and have never seen the written edition.

Accuracy rules (these override style):
- Use ONLY the facts, names, numbers, sources and opinions in the sections. Never add a fact, a number, a company, a person, a publication or a view. If a section is short, its script is short.
- Keep every figure exactly as it is written in the section: "$20B", "22%", "500 MW", "GPT-6.1", "52 to 53". Do not spell figures out as words and do not round or convert them. A separate step turns figures into speech, so a figure written in words cannot be checked and will be rejected.
- Quotation: put words in quotation marks only when the same words are in quotation marks in the section, exactly as they appear there. When you use one, bring it in the way a good presenter would, and vary it: "in his words", "as the paper puts it", "what she called", "his phrase was", or just let the attribution lead into it. Never say "quote" or "unquote", and never introduce two quotations the same way. Paraphrase everything else in plain words and attribute it ("according to Techmeme", "The Batch reports", "Marcus argues").
- Only say how well a claim holds up for a story whose section has a "Critical pass" line (the written edition calls it the evidence check), and only give our view where there is an "Our read" (or "Editor's view") line. Make clear when the episode moves from what happened, to how well it is supported, to what we think, but say it naturally and vary the wording from story to story; do not repeat one formula six times.
- Suggest, don't instruct: implications are observations and questions ("a question worth asking might be...", "we'd want to see..."), never orders to the listener ("ask every founder", "stop accepting", "treat this as"). Keep the view clear.
- Our read is an editorial "we", never "I". When a section has another view ("The AGI-forward view", "The AI-realist view", "The market view", "The policy view"), give it fairly as the way others read the same facts ("the AGI-forward reading is...", "from an AI-realist angle, ..."), after our read, so the listener hears that the field is moving and the evidence is contested. These are ways of reading evidence, not teams: never "camp", "side", "believers", "doomers" or "hype crowd", and never "sceptic" as a label for a view.
- Do not say how many stories there are in total ("the first of five"): a story can be dropped after checking, so use plain ordinals ("first", "second", "third", "the last of the main stories"); do not start more than one story with "next".
- Never mention Steven, his board seats, conflicts of interest or companies he follows; that context is private and only means reading those stories more critically.
- Do not greet anyone by name and do not open the episode: the first line, in which the host names the episode and introduces themselves ("This is the AI Briefing for ... I'm ..."), is added separately, so never repeat the host's name or the date. Do not close it either: the sign-off is added separately.
- No markdown, no bullet points, no URLs, no item numbers, no parentheses, no "click" or "see below".
- For the ear: no sentence over 30 words; at most two numbers in a sentence; name at most three companies or people in a list and then say "and N others"; name at most two outlets per story.
- Dates: when a story's Sources line says "from Fri Oct 9", the item is older than today's news: say so in relative words ("in last Friday's issue of The Batch") near the start of that story. Never read a date in digits.
